# Personal PRFAQ

## What this is

A **PRFAQ** (Press Release + Frequently Asked Questions) is a planning document
made famous by the "Working Backwards" method: instead of starting with what you
can do today and pushing forward, you write the announcement of a finished
success first, then work backwards to figure out how to make it real.

This skill adapts that method for **you as a person**. The "product" you are
launching is a version of your own life — a goal reached, a habit built, a fear
outgrown, a relationship healed, a business started, a self you are becoming.

You write it in two parts:

1. **The Press Release** — a short, vivid announcement, dated in the future,
   describing the change as if it has *already happened*.
2. **The FAQ** — honest questions and answers that map the journey: what was in
   the way, what you actually did, and what had to be true for it to work.

The magic is not positive thinking. It is the **forcing function**: if you
cannot write a compelling, specific press release about your future, the vision
is not clear enough yet. And if the FAQ has no hard questions in it, you are
selling yourself a story instead of making a plan.

## Core principles

Carry these into every section. They are what separate a real Personal PRFAQ
from a vision board.

- **Work backwards from the future, not forwards from today.** Start with the
  finished result and the person you've become. Do not start with your current
  habits, resources, or limitations and ask "what can I realistically do." That
  comes later, in the FAQ.
- **Be specific, not aspirational.** "I'm healthier and happier" is a wish.
  "I run a 5K three mornings a week and sleep 7 hours" is a destination. Vague
  visions cannot be planned or measured.
- **Write for one reader: your future self.** Just as a product press release
  targets one customer segment, your PRFAQ targets one specific version of you,
  at one specific time. Name the date.
- **Truth-seeking, not selling.** The goal is not to hype yourself up or "win"
  approval. It is to find out what is actually true — what will really stand in
  your way and what will really have to change. An honest FAQ is the whole point.
- **Name the current alternative.** You already cope with this gap somehow
  (avoidance, a temporary fix, a story you tell yourself). Be clear about what
  you do today and why the new way is genuinely better.
- **Optimistic but realistic.** Don't look through rose-colored glasses. A good
  PRFAQ includes the risks, the likely failure points, and the conditions under
  which this succeeds or fails.
- **Show the artifact early.** Do not keep someone answering questions for a
  long time before they see a draft. A quick first draft with visible holes is
  better than a perfect intake process.
- **Make the process interactive.** Between question rounds, give the person a
  tiny useful output: a one-line recap, a few keywords, or a light "I'm
  hearing..." reflection. Keep the momentum moving; do not over-iterate between
  question batches.
- **Show progress.** At the start of each question batch, include a lightweight
  progress tracker so the person knows where they are in the current path.
- **Iterate.** The quick version should produce a useful draft in about 20
  minutes. The full version may take 60-90 minutes or several sittings. Fidelity
  increases with each draft.

## How to guide someone through it

Start by offering two paths:

1. **Quick 20-minute version** — ask only the essential questions, then produce a
   rough but useful Personal PRFAQ draft with clearly marked assumptions and
   holes.
2. **Full version** — guide the person through the complete PRFAQ, still showing
   partial drafts along the way instead of waiting until the end.

At the beginning of either path, ask for the person's preferred name. Use that
name in the document title or headline area, such as `[Name]'s Personal PRFAQ` or
`[Name] Built...`, unless the person asks to keep the document anonymous.

If the person is unsure, choose the quick version. In either path, do not ask
more than two clusters of questions without showing a draft, synthesis, or
partial PRFAQ section. Avoid making the person answer questions for more than
about 20 minutes before they see something concrete. If the person has already
answered enough to infer a rough draft, draft immediately and mark assumptions
instead of asking more setup questions.

Between question clusters, provide a tiny intermediate output before asking the
next cluster. Keep it to one or two lines. Good examples:
- `I'm hearing: founder, AI anxiety, helping people feel steady. Keep going.`
- `Keywords so far: freedom, new game, working parents, AI uncertainty.`
- `Current shape: not productivity for its own sake — helping humans settle into
  the AI age.`

Use fuller draft fragments only when they naturally help, such as after enough
answers for a headline or short paragraph. Avoid turning every pause into a
mini-analysis.

Use a simple text progress tracker with each question batch. Keep it approximate
and human, not overly precise. Examples:
- `Quick draft progress: [##---] 40% — framing and audience`
- `Full PRFAQ progress: [###-----] 35% — press release draft`
- `Batch 1 of 2: [##---] 40% — answer these, then I'll show a draft frame`

For the quick version, treat the two question rounds plus draft as the main
progress arc:
- `0%` when choosing the path and starting the frame.
- `40%` after the first question batch and brief recap.
- `70%` after the final question batch, while preparing the draft.
- `100%` when the draft is shown or the HTML file is created.

Do not show the completed draft while still labeling progress as 70%. If the
draft appears in the response, progress should be 100% or "draft complete." For
the full version, track the major sections: frame, press release, FAQ,
pressure-test, final draft.

The full question set lives in `reference/questions.md`. The fill-in-the-blank
document structure lives in `reference/template.md`. A worked example lives in
`reference/example.md`. The standalone HTML output template lives in
`reference/html-template.html`. Read those files when you need the detail; the
flow below is the spine.

### Quick 20-minute version
Use this when the person wants momentum, seems impatient, is exploring a vague
goal, or has not explicitly chosen the full version.

Ask for the name, then ask about 6-8 core content questions in no more than two
rounds:
- What name should appear on the PRFAQ?
- What is the one change or future outcome?
- What date should this be true by?
- Why does it matter now?
- Who benefits, and what is different for them?
- What is painful, stuck, or missing today?
- What would make this feel real: evidence, numbers, behavior, impact, or
  financial outcome?
- What are the top reasons this might not happen?
- Is this genuinely the person's own definition of success?

After the first 3-4 answers, pause with a brief recap or keywords, then ask the
remaining quick-path questions.

After the final question batch and before producing the artifact, explicitly
signal the transition: `Info collection finished. Generating your Personal
PRFAQ.`

Then produce a draft with:
- Headline
- Subheadline
- Short summary paragraph
- Gap paragraph
- Change paragraph
- 3-5 FAQ questions, including the top three reasons this might not happen

Use placeholders where needed instead of asking another long round of questions.
After the draft, ask whether they want to revise the draft, deepen the FAQ, or
switch into the full version.

### Full version
Use this when the person explicitly wants depth, polish, or a complete planning
document.

Work through the sections **in order**. Ask the questions conversationally — one
cluster at a time — and let the person answer before moving on. Don't dump every
question at once. Reflect their answers back, push gently where things are vague,
and draft partial sections as soon as there is enough material. After each
cluster, show a brief recap before asking the next cluster.

### Step 1 — Set the frame
Before any writing, establish three things:
- **The person's name.** Ask what name should appear on the PRFAQ. Use it in the
  document title or headline area unless they prefer anonymity.
- **The one change.** What single area of life or specific goal is this PRFAQ
  about? Keep it to one. If they name several, help them pick the one that
  matters most right now, or write separate PRFAQs.
- **The launch date.** Pick a real target date — the day this is true. Six
  months, one year, three years. The date should mean something and be
  believable enough to plan around.

### Step 2 — Draft the Press Release
Work through, in this order (see `reference/questions.md` §1–5):
- **Headline** — the announcement in one line.
- **Subheadline** — who this serves (you, and who else benefits) and the core
  benefit, in one line.
- **Summary paragraph** — dated opener + a plain-language summary of what changed
  and why it matters.
- **The gap paragraph** — the problem, written from the perspective of who you
  are *today*. What was missing, costing you, or holding you back.
- **The transformation paragraph(s)** — who you became and the concrete systems,
  habits, decisions, and support that made it real. This must directly answer
  the gap.
- **Two quotes + getting started** — a quote from your future self on what it
  feels like now, a quote from someone your change touched, and the honest first
  step you took to begin (see `reference/questions.md` §6).

### Step 3 — Write the FAQ
This is where honesty lives. Cover both:
- **External FAQ** — what other people would ask or notice about the change (see
  `reference/questions.md` §7).
- **Internal FAQ** — the hard questions you ask yourself: the real obstacles,
  what you're giving up, what has to be true, how you'll handle setbacks, and the
  top three reasons this might *not* happen (see `reference/questions.md` §8).

A PRFAQ with no uncomfortable questions in the internal FAQ is not finished.

### Step 4 — Pressure-test and iterate
Read it back together and check:
- Is the future self **specific** enough to picture and measure?
- Does the transformation paragraph actually solve the gap paragraph?
- Is it different from what they've already tried — and is that difference real?
- Does the internal FAQ name genuine risks, not token ones?
- Would they actually *want* this life, or is it someone else's definition of
  success?

Then invite a second draft. Note what still has holes and what to sit with.

## Output

For the quick version, produce a compact draft that follows the spirit of
`reference/template.md` but may be shorter and include placeholders or open
questions.

For the full version, produce the finished Personal PRFAQ as a clean document
following `reference/template.md`. Keep the press release to about one page —
tight and vivid. Let the FAQ be as long as honesty requires.

When the environment allows creating files, the primary deliverable should be a
standalone HTML file, not only text in the chat. Use
`reference/html-template.html` as the visual model and create a polished
press-release-style document that can be opened in a browser. The chat response
should summarize what was created and link or point to the file, rather than
duplicating the whole PRFAQ.

Before creating the final HTML or final draft, say: `Info collection finished.
Generating your Personal PRFAQ.` Use equivalent wording only if it keeps the same
clear handoff from questions to output.

Offer Markdown only as an optional companion format for easier editing; do not
make Markdown the default final artifact when HTML file creation is available.

HTML output requirements:
- Use a single `.html` file with embedded CSS and no external dependencies.
- Make it look like a real press release: clear masthead, release date,
  headline, subheadline, dateline, body copy, pull quotes, and FAQ section.
- Include the person's name in the title or headline area unless they asked for
  anonymity.
- Preserve the PRFAQ structure and honesty. A polished layout must not hide
  unresolved questions, risks, or placeholders.
- Name the file descriptively, such as `personal-prfaq-founder-2031.html` or
  `personal-prfaq-draft.html`.
- If file creation is not available, provide the complete standalone HTML in a
  code block so the person can save or adapt it in their own environment.

Keep follow-up questions outside the HTML artifact. After delivering the file,
show a short optional continuation note in chat:

`If you want to keep going, here are more questions, but we can stop here and
revisit whenever you want to.`

Then list up to 3-5 useful next questions. Make clear the user can stop at any
time and return later.

## Tone

This is personal and can bring up real emotion. Be warm, curious, and
non-judgmental. Don't lecture, don't cheerlead, and don't hand them clichés.
Ask good questions, listen to the answer, and help them tell the truth about
what they want and what it will take.
