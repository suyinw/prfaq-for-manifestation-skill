# Personal PRFAQ — The Questions

Ask these conversationally, a cluster at a time. They are grouped to match the
structure of the document. Not every question needs a written answer — they are
prompts to think and talk through. Follow the person's energy, and always push
from vague toward specific.

For a quick first pass, do not use the whole list. Ask only enough to create a
visible draft, then mark what still needs thought.

Between question clusters, show a tiny intermediate output before asking more:
a one-line recap, a few keywords, or a light "I'm hearing..." reflection. Do not
over-iterate between batches.

Start each question batch with a lightweight progress tracker, such as:
`Quick draft progress: [##---] 40% — framing and audience`.

---

## Quick 20-minute path

Use this path when the person wants momentum or has not explicitly chosen the
full version. Ask for the name, then about 6-8 core content questions before
drafting something.

1. What name should appear on the PRFAQ?
2. What is the one change or future outcome this PRFAQ is about?
3. What date should this be true by, and why that date?
4. Why does this matter now, rather than someday?
5. Who benefits from this change, and what is different for them?
6. What is painful, stuck, or missing today?
7. What would make this feel real: evidence, numbers, behavior, impact, revenue,
   freedom, or something else?
8. What are the top three reasons this might not happen?
9. Is this genuinely your definition of success, or someone else's?

After these answers, draft a compact PRFAQ with assumptions and holes clearly
marked. Before drafting, say: "Info collection finished. Generating your
Personal PRFAQ." Continue with the full question set only if the person wants to
deepen the draft.

---

## §0 — Framing (before you write anything)

1. What name should appear on the PRFAQ?
2. If you could wake up one day and one thing about your life had genuinely
   changed, what would it be?
3. Is this one change, or several tangled together? If several, which one, if it
   came true, would make the others easier or matter most?
4. What is the date this is true? Pick a real day — a birthday, a season, a year
   from now. Why that date?
5. Why does this matter *now*? What makes this the moment, rather than "someday"?

---

## §1 — Headline (the announcement)

5. Say it as a one-line announcement, in the present or past tense, as if it has
   already happened. ("I finished my first marathon." "I left the job that was
   draining me and I run my own studio.")
6. Does the headline name a real, finished outcome — not an intention? If it
   starts with "I want to" or "I'm trying to," rewrite it as done.

---

## §2 — Subheadline (who it serves and the core benefit)

7. Who is the "you" this is for? Describe that future version of yourself
   specifically — how they spend their days, how they feel, what they no longer
   worry about.
8. Beyond you, whose life gets better because you made this change? (A partner,
   kids, friends, community, people you'll go on to help.)
9. In one sentence: what is the single most important benefit of this change?

---

## §3 — Summary paragraph

10. Imagine the date has arrived. Describe, in a few plain sentences, what is
    true now that wasn't before.
11. If a close friend who hadn't seen you in a year met you on that date, what
    would they immediately notice is different?
12. What does an ordinary day look like in this future? Walk through it.

---

## §4 — The gap (the problem, from today's point of view)

13. What is missing, stuck, or painful in this area of your life right now?
14. What is it actually costing you to leave it unchanged — in time, energy,
    health, relationships, money, or peace of mind?
15. How do you cope with or work around this gap today? (Everyone has a current
    "solution," even if it's avoidance, distraction, or a temporary fix.)
16. What have you already tried, and where did it fall short?
17. What story do you tell yourself about why it hasn't changed yet? Is that
    story true?

---

## §5 — The transformation (who you became and how)

18. Who did you have to *become* for this to be true? What changed in how you
    think, decide, or show up?
19. What specific habits, routines, or systems made the difference? Be concrete —
    daily, weekly, the actual mechanics.
20. What did you stop doing? (Change is often subtraction as much as addition.)
21. What decision or turning point set it in motion?
22. Who or what supported you — people, tools, environments, communities?
23. In what way is this path genuinely different from how you tried before, or
    from how most people approach it? Why does *this* way work when others didn't?

---

## §6 — Quotes and the first step

24. In your future self's own words, what does it feel like to be here now? Say
    it as a quote.
25. Someone whose life your change touched — what would they say about the
    difference it made? Say it as a quote from them.
26. Looking back, what was the very first, unglamorous step you took to begin?
27. How hard was it to start, honestly — and what made it possible to take that
    first step anyway?

---

## §7 — External FAQ (what others would ask or notice)

28. What would people who know you be most surprised by?
29. What would they ask about how you did it?
30. What would skeptics say — and what's your honest answer?
31. How would you explain the change to someone who wants to do the same thing?

---

## §8 — Internal FAQ (the honest, hard questions to yourself)

These are the most important questions in the whole document. Do not skip them.
A PRFAQ that only contains encouraging answers is a story you're selling
yourself, not a plan.

32. What has to be true for this to actually happen? List the real conditions.
33. What is the hardest problem you'll have to solve along the way?
34. What are you going to have to give up, say no to, or let go of?
35. Who might you disappoint, and can you live with that?
36. What has stopped you before, and what's different this time — really?
37. When you hit a setback or fall off, how will you get back on? (Assume you
    will.)
38. What are the **top three reasons this might not happen**? Be brutally honest.
39. What's the smallest version of this you could commit to, that would still be
    real progress?
40. Is this genuinely *your* definition of a life you want — or someone else's
    idea of success you've absorbed? How do you know?

---

## Facilitation notes

- When an answer is vague ("be happier," "get in shape," "be successful"), ask
  "what would that look like on an ordinary Tuesday?" until it's concrete.
- When an answer is all upside, gently ask for the cost or the risk.
- Reflect answers back in the person's own words before drafting.
- It's fine to leave holes in a first pass. Mark them and come back.
- Do not make the person answer questions for more than about 20 minutes before
  showing a draft, synthesis, or partial PRFAQ section.
- After each question cluster, give an intermediate output so the person can
  react, correct the direction, and feel the document taking shape. Keep this
  lightweight: keywords or one sentence is often enough.
- Use an approximate progress tracker with each question batch. Prefer simple
  labels like `Batch 1 of 2`, `40%`, or `[##---]` over precise scoring.
- In the quick path, use 70% only while preparing the draft. Once the draft is
  shown, label progress as 100% or draft complete.
