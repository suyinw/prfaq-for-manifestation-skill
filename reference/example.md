# Personal PRFAQ — Worked Example

_A filled-in example to show the level of specificity and honesty to aim for.
The person here is a fictional 41-year-old who wants to leave a draining job and
start a small woodworking business. Use it as a reference for tone and depth, not
a script to copy._

---

## PART 1 — THE PRESS RELEASE

**FOR RELEASE: October 2027**

### I left the job that was hollowing me out and now build furniture people keep for life

#### How a stressed-out operations manager became a self-employed woodworker — earning enough, sleeping better, and present for my family again

**Portland, October 2027** — Two years ago I dreaded Mondays. Today I walked into
my own workshop, put on a record, and started on a walnut dining table a family
commissioned for their first home. I replaced most of my old salary, I work with
my hands, and I haven't taken work anxiety to bed in months. It's not bigger or
flashier than my old life. It's mine.

**The gap.** I was an operations manager making good money and slowly
disappearing. The stress followed me home; I was short with my kids and numb on
weekends. I coped by telling myself "everyone hates their job" and by promising
I'd figure it out "next year." I'd taken a couple of weekend woodworking classes
and loved them, but I never treated it as anything but a hobby because it felt
irresponsible.

**The change.** I stopped waiting for a perfect leap. For a year I built the
business on the side: two evenings and one weekend morning a week in a rented
shared shop, one commission at a time, reinvesting everything. I saved a
nine-month runway before quitting. I found a mentor who'd made the same
transition and I followed his pricing advice instead of undercharging. I learned
the boring half — bookkeeping, contracts, marketing on Instagram — because that's
what actually made it a business and not a hobby. When I had six months of
steady commissions booked, I gave notice.

> "I was terrified I'd regret leaving a stable job. What I actually regret is how
> long I waited to trust myself." — me, October 2027

> "Dad's just... around now. He laughs more." — my daughter, age 12

**Getting started was simple.** I booked one shared-shop session for a Tuesday
night and told one friend I was doing it, so I couldn't quietly back out. That
was the whole first step.

## PART 2 — THE FAQ

### External FAQ

**Q: What would surprise people most about this change?**
That it wasn't a dramatic leap. It was two years of small, unglamorous evenings
before anything visible happened.

**Q: How did you actually do it?**
Side income first, a real cash runway second, and only then the resignation. Never
a single all-or-nothing bet.

**Q: What would a skeptic say — and what's your honest response?**
"Most small businesses fail." True. That's exactly why I validated demand with
paying customers for a year before I quit, instead of after.

**Q: What would you tell someone who wants to do the same thing?**
Don't quit first. Build the evidence that it works while you still have a
paycheck, then jump.

### Internal FAQ

**Q: What has to be true for this to actually happen?**
I need ~9 months of expenses saved, a repeatable way to find 2–3 commissions a
month, and my partner fully on board with the risk and the tighter budget.

**Q: What is the hardest problem you'll have to solve?**
Consistent customer flow. Building is the easy part; being found and booked is
the hard part.

**Q: What are you going to have to give up, say no to, or let go of?**
The salary, the identity of a "safe" career, some comfort spending, and the
approval of relatives who think this is reckless.

**Q: What has stopped you before — and what is genuinely different this time?**
Fear dressed up as "responsibility." What's different is I've now made real money
from it and have a runway, so the fear has fewer facts to stand on.

**Q: When you hit a setback (a dry month, a botched piece), how will you get back on?**
A three-month cash buffer inside the runway so one bad month isn't a crisis, and
a standing monthly call with my mentor so I don't spiral alone.

**Q: What are the top three reasons this might NOT happen?**
1. I never build the savings runway and stay "safe" out of fear.
2. I can't find customers reliably and income is too lumpy to live on.
3. My partner and I aren't truly aligned on the financial risk.

**Q: What's the smallest version that would still be real progress?**
Keep the day job and run it as a serious side business clearing $1,500/month.
Even that would prove the model and change how I feel about my life.

**Q: Is this genuinely the life YOU want — or someone else's idea of success?**
Mine. Nobody around me is pushing me toward this — most are nervous about it. The
pull is entirely internal, which is how I know it's real.
